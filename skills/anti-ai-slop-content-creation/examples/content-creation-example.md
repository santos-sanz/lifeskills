# Anti-AI-slop content example

## Context and audience
- Audience: Builders who are tired of generic productivity advice.
- Purpose and channel: Short professional post that shares one practical lesson from a failed launch.
- Length and format: 180 words, plain text with a useful ending.
- Desired reader action: Check one dependency before promising a launch date.

## Core point and human angle
- Central point: A launch date became credible only after the team named the dependency that could break it.
- Concrete scene, tension, choice, or consequence: In the launch review, everyone agreed on the date until someone asked who owned the missing security approval.
- Voice constraints: Direct, specific, modest; no motivational slogans.

## Draft
The launch date looked solid until someone asked a boring question: who owns the security approval?

No one had a name. The date was not a plan yet; it was a shared hope with a calendar invite.

We wrote down the dependency, assigned an owner, and added a fallback review path. That made the conversation less exciting and the date more credible. It also showed us that the work was not “blocked by security.” It was blocked by an unnamed decision and no deadline.

The useful launch habit is simple: before promising a date, ask which dependency can still break it, who owns it, and what happens if it slips.

## Anti-slop audit
- Generic or reusable sentence removed: Replaced “alignment drives execution” with the unnamed approval scene.
- Unsupported detail removed or marked: Removed the unverified two-week saving.
- Repetition or filler removed: Deleted a broad productivity introduction.
- Remaining sentence needing confirmation: Confirm that the security approval was the actual critical path.

## Verification and attribution
- Claims to verify: The approval dependency and its effect on the launch date.
- Sources or attribution: User-provided anonymized project note; no external source claimed.
- Privacy, consent, or disclosure note: Do not include company, customer, or person names without consent.
