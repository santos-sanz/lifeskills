# Difficult conversation example

## Context and goal
- Relationship and context: A project lead repeatedly changes acceptance criteria after review, creating rework for a designer.
- Conversation goal: Agree a stable review rule and a respectful escalation path.
- Desired outcome: Changes after sign-off are logged as new scope with an owner and impact.

## Observations and impact
- Observable fact 1: The acceptance checklist changed twice after the design review on 2026-08-18.
- Impact: The designer repeated 12 hours of work and the release date moved by two days.
- Interpretation to avoid treating as fact: The lead does not respect design or is intentionally creating churn.

## Conversation plan
- Format and setting: Private 25-minute video call, followed by a short written summary.
- Opening: “I want to make review changes easier to handle without blaming either of us.”
- Listening question: “What made the late changes necessary, and what constraint did I miss?”
- Specific request: “Can we freeze acceptance criteria at review and log later changes as new scope?”

## Script and listening prompts
- Opening script: “When the checklist changed twice after sign-off, the work was repeated and the release moved. I want to agree a rule that protects quality and timing.”
- Question 1: “What would make that rule workable for your stakeholders?”
- Reflection or summary: “You need room for compliance changes, while I need changes to show their impact. Is that right?”
- Repair or pause line: “I want to solve the process, not assign intent. Can we pause and return with the missing constraint?”

## Response branches
- If they agree: Confirm the checklist owner, change log, and next review date in writing.
- If they disagree: Ask for one concrete exception and compare it with a time-boxed change-control rule.
- If they become distressed or angry: Pause, acknowledge impact, and offer to continue with a manager or support person if safe.
- If there is denial or no change: Record the observable examples and use the agreed project escalation channel.

## Boundary or request
- Boundary or request: After sign-off, new acceptance criteria require a change note, impact estimate, and owner.
- Support needed: Product manager owns the change log; project lead confirms the rule at the next review.
- Condition for escalation: A further unlogged change moves the committed release or creates a quality risk.

## Follow-up
- Action: Publish the review and change-control rule.
- Owner: Project Lead.
- Due date: 2026-08-29.
- Success signal: Next review has one signed checklist and every later change has an owner and impact.

## Risks and safety
- Power or safety risk: The project lead controls the designer's priorities but no immediate safety risk is known.
- Mitigation or support channel: Keep the conversation private, factual, and escalate through the project manager if retaliation or repeated scope abuse appears.
- Confidentiality limit: Share only the examples needed for the project record.

## Assumptions
- Assumption: The team has a documented review owner and escalation path.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Validation needed: Confirm decision rights before the conversation.
