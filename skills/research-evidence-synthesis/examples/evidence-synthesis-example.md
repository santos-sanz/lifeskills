# Evidence synthesis example

## Research question and scope
- Research question: Should a 60-person SaaS team adopt a four-day meeting-free block each week?
- Decision or audience: COO decision for the next quarter.
- Geography, population, period, and definitions: Internal product and engineering teams; next quarter; productivity means completed planned work without increased incidents.
- Freshness requirement: Evidence from the last three years where available.

## Executive synthesis
- A protected meeting-free block is a reasonable reversible pilot, not a proven permanent policy. Confidence: Medium.
- Internal baseline and a time-boxed pilot matter more than generic productivity claims because team composition and coordination load differ.

## Evidence ledger
| Claim | Source and URL | Published/accessed | Quality | Confidence | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal teams report that recurring meetings fragment focus time. | Illustrative internal pulse survey record (example data) | 2026-08-10 / 2026-08-27 | Primary | Medium | Self-reported and not causal. |
| A meeting-free block can reduce interruption exposure in a comparable team. | Unverified external summary (no source used for the recommendation) | 2025-11-04 / 2026-08-27 | Secondary | Low-Medium | Different team size and workflow; URL not verified. |

## Conflicts and gaps
- Conflict: Teams value fewer meetings but report slower cross-team decisions during protected focus periods.
- Most plausible explanation: Coordination cost depends on exception handling and decision rights, not meeting count alone.
- Unknown that could change the conclusion: Whether incident response and customer escalations can be routed asynchronously.

## Implications or recommendation
- Run a four-week pilot with explicit exception rules, a shared decision log, and a rollback condition if cycle time or incidents worsen.
- This is an Inference from the available evidence, not a proven causal result.
- Change condition: stop or redesign if cycle time rises more than 10 percent or urgent work lacks an owner.

## Next actions
- Action: Define pilot scope, exception policy, and baseline metrics.
- Owner: COO and Engineering Lead.
- Due date: 2026-09-03.
- Success signal: Approved pilot brief with baseline cycle time, incident count, and escalation owner.

## Sources and freshness
- Primary sources: Internal pulse survey and delivery metrics; accessed 2026-08-27.
- Supporting sources: None verified; the comparable-team summary is explicitly unverified.
- Stale or unavailable evidence: No causal external study matching this team's workflow was found.

## Assumptions
- Assumption: The team can measure cycle time and incidents consistently during the pilot.
- Confidence: Medium.
- Validation needed: Metrics audit before launch.
