# Evidence protocol

## Source hierarchy
- Primary: original dataset, filing, experiment, official record, interview, or first-party documentation.
- Authoritative secondary: reputable research institution, standards body, regulator, or specialist analysis with a stated method.
- Contextual secondary: journalism, commentary, aggregators, and summaries used for discovery or context.
- Unverified: anonymous, undated, promotional, or inaccessible material that cannot support a material claim alone.

## Claim ledger fields
For each material claim record:
- Claim wording
- Source title and URL
- Publisher or author
- Publication date and access date
- Population, geography, period, and method
- Evidence quality and confidence
- Limitation or caveat

## Triangulation rules
- Compare independent sources, not multiple copies of the same claim.
- Reconcile different definitions and denominators before comparing numbers.
- If sources conflict, preserve the conflict and explain the most plausible cause.
- Do not average incompatible estimates without a defensible method.

## Freshness rules
- Current policy, prices, schedules, products, people, and market conditions require a recent access check.
- Historical claims should retain their original date and should not be presented as current.
- State when no sufficiently recent source was found.
