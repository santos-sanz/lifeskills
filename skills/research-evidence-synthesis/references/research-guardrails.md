# Research guardrails

## Citation integrity
- Never fabricate citations, quotes, URLs, publication dates, or source conclusions.
- Cite the source that directly supports the claim; do not cite a search-result page as the evidence.
- Keep quotations short and prefer a faithful paraphrase with a direct link.

## Privacy and access
- Do not collect, expose, or infer sensitive personal data that is not necessary for the question.
- Respect access controls, paywalls, robots, and terms of use.
- Distinguish public information from private or user-provided information.

## High-stakes downgrade
- For legal, medical, financial, safety, or regulatory decisions, provide evidence and uncertainty without pretending to replace qualified advice.
- Name the professional review or primary authority needed before action.

## Failure checks
- Could the conclusion change if the source is stale?
- Does the evidence actually measure the user's population and outcome?
- Is a confident sentence hiding a material unknown?
